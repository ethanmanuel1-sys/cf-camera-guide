PART 1 · 3 MIN

Start with your why

Why this church? Why this team? Why give up a weekend morning? Write your answer down and keep it where you’ll see it. On long weekends and early call times, your why is what keeps the camera steady.

HEART CHECK

No matter how hard we work, and no matter what mistakes get made live, there is always somebody on the other end of the lens being touched.

Chad, our broadcast coach

10% tech, 90% heart

This is worship, not just production. The camera is your instrument the way drums are the drummer’s. You are loved for who you are, not what you do. Serve from that, not for it.

A safe place to fail

  • Live production guarantees mistakes, for ten-year veterans too. A missed shot never stopped God from moving.
  • You’ll be asked to try shots you might not land. That’s on purpose. It’s how you grow.
  • Don’t go home and beat yourself up. Rewatch, learn, move on.

PART 3 · 5 MIN

Framing

Thirds, never center (almost)

Centered is for people talking to the camera. Worship subjects go on the left or right third. One exception: a tight instrument shot, like hands on keys, can sit centered.

The mic-hand rule

Whichever hand holds the mic decides the side. Mic in the right hand puts the singer on the left third, so when the free hand lifts in worship it fills the frame instead of leaving it.

Looking room

Leave open space on the side the subject faces. Even empty space feels right at home. Pressed against the edge, they look like they’re staring out of the viewer’s TV.

Headroom: less than you think

The most common note on new operators’ footage. Bring the top of frame down. When in doubt, tighter on top.

PART 4 · 4 MIN

The Bounce & Cinema Verité

On sticks

Loosen the tripod head a little and trace a slow figure-eight with your crosshairs. With practice it turns into organic drift, like a ball on a rubber band floating inside a circle. The shot never sits dead.

Handheld

Hold the camera wherever it’s comfortable, chest or shoulder, and move with the music. Fast song, faster bounce. Slow song, slower. Walking around a player, bend your knees and step heel to toe: your legs absorb the motion and your upper body floats like a gimbal.

Replace every zoom with a reset

No slow push-ins or pull-outs. The moment the Camera Director cuts away from you, snap to a new frame: tighter, wider, or a new subject. Tight, off, head-to-toe, off, medium. There’s always a fresh shot to come back to.

THE #1 NOTE ON OUR FOOTAGE

“Good move. 5 to 10% faster.” On builds, go over the top. The Camera Director can dial you down with “less, less… pocket that.” Timid can’t be dialed up.

Level 2: match the singer, not the band

When the band builds but the vocalist stays even and unhurried, follow the singer. It will feel too slow. That’s how you know it’s right.

PART 5 · 3 MIN

The core rules

  1. Stay take-able 100% of the timeSOFTKeep a usable frame even mid-move. The Camera Director can come back to you at any second.
  2. Be intentionalKnow what you’re shooting before you move. Searching on camera reads as a mistake at home.
  3. Know your end frameLand the move so clean you could print it and hang it. If something goes wrong mid-move, go back to your last good shot.
  4. Know your zoneTotal creative freedom inside it. Stay out of everyone else’s unless you’re called.
  5. Listen activelyProgram, guide and click are in your ears on purpose. If a part starts and its camera is live, the nearest camera covers it without being asked.
  6. Chain your shotsTwo-in-ones now: a rack focus, a pan down, hands to face. Three-in-ones as you grow. Drill them at soundcheck first.

PART 7 · 4 MIN

What your Camera Director is looking for

Understand the chair on the other end of comms and you’ll operate two levels better.

  • Cuts land on the music. Chord changes and band hits, not a fixed count. Live inside the song and your resets will be ready when a cut needs somewhere to go.
  • Build, then breathe. Fast cuts into the big moment, a wide at the peak, then a hold on one operator. If that hold is on you, keep the shot evolving.
  • No cuts to a player who isn’t playing yet. If you hear “coming to you soon,” frame it and hold ready.
  • Never the same subject twice in a row. Just left the bass? Offer something else first.
  • Mistakes don’t get called out on comms. Corrections come as “try the left third… yes, perfect, thank you.” That’s coaching.
  • “Lock that in” means remember the feel. It’s your new baseline for that tempo.

The banned list

These read as old-school broadcast. If leadership loves one, leadership wins.

Slow push-ins and pull-outsinsteadBounce, plus a reset between cuts
Slow pansinsteadWhip on the beat, or hold the third
Auto Trans (dissolves)insteadCut. Even on slow songs.
Dutch angles and barrel rollsinsteadMovement, racks and reveals
Centered wide or medium subjectsinsteadThirds and looking room
